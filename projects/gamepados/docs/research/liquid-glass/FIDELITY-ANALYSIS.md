# Liquid Glass — fidelity gap analysis

How close can an Android WebView actually get to iOS 26's material, behaviour by
behaviour. Compiled 2026-07-28 from Apple HIG / WWDC25 session 219 / Apple Newsroom,
plus two working prototypes in this folder:

- `refraction-lab.html` — Snell's-law edge lensing, measured
- `specular-lab.html` — gyro-driven rim highlight, measured

Verdict key: **DONE** = prototyped and verified here · **VIABLE** = known method, not yet
built · **PARTIAL** = approximable, not faithful · **BLOCKED** = not reachable on this platform.

---

## 1. The optical core

| # | Behaviour | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Edge refraction (lensing) | **DONE** | Snell's law → displacement map → `feDisplacementMap`. Verified: centre `[128,128]`, all edges displace inward, 28% of panel affected. |
| 1.2 | Squircle bezel profile | **DONE** | `y=(1−(1−x)⁴)^¼`. A circular profile leaves a visible kink at the flat→curve join. |
| 1.3 | Chromatic aberration | **VIABLE** | Displace R and B at slightly different `scale`, recombine with `feBlend mode="screen"`. Wired in the lab, not yet tuned. |
| 1.4 | Backdrop blur + saturate | **DONE** | `blur() saturate(180%)`. Skipping `saturate` is why most CSS glass looks like frosted plastic. |
| 1.5 | Specular rim highlight | **DONE** | Masked blob inside a 1.4px ring. |
| 1.6 | Highlight tracks device motion | **DONE** | Translate the blob by tilt. Compositor-only. |
| 1.7 | True squircle corners | **VIABLE** | `corner-shape: squircle` — confirmed supported in Chromium. Progressive enhancement; degrades to `border-radius`. |
| 1.8 | Real-time environment sampling | **BLOCKED** | Apple's material is a live GPU shader reading the framebuffer. `backdrop-filter` samples the backdrop but we cannot read it back to drive other properties without a compositing round-trip. |

**The architectural finding.** Refraction costs 13–33 ms to build; the highlight must move
every frame. They cannot be the same asset. Split them:

- **refraction** → static, built once at mount, cached as a data URL
- **specular** → a translated blob, zero rebuild

That split is what makes the whole thing affordable, and it fell straight out of measuring.

## 2. Interaction

| # | Behaviour | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Press → gel compress | **DONE** | `scale(.968)`, `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)` over .22s. Verified. |

## 3. Adaptive behaviour

| # | Behaviour | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Larger element ⇒ thicker material | **DONE** | Shadow and blur scale with size. Measured: pill 190px → `0 4px 14px`/blur 6; card 300px → `0 8px 28px`/blur 10; sheet 430px → `0 16px 46px`/blur 16. |
| 3.2 | Shadow deepens as content scrolls under | **VIABLE** | Cheap: scroll listener → raise shadow alpha. One of the highest ratios of perceived polish to effort. |
| 3.3 | Auto light/dark per component | **PARTIAL** | Apple decides per-component from what's behind. We can only switch on the app theme, not on local backdrop luminance. |
| 3.4 | Colour spill from nearby content | **BLOCKED (faithful)** / **PARTIAL** | Faithful version needs framebuffer reads. Approximation: sample the known backdrop colour in JS and tint the fill. Costs a canvas read — do it once on open, never per frame. |
| 3.5 | Regular vs Clear variants | **DONE** | Both in `tokens.css`. Clear is only legal over media, with a scrim, and bold content on top. |

## 3b. Motion — the other half of "feeling Apple"

Prototyped in `motion-lab.html`; generated by `gen-motion.mjs` into `motion.css`.

**The finding that changes everything here: iOS does not animate on bezier curves.**
It animates on **springs**. A `cubic-bezier` is monotonic by construction — it physically
cannot overshoot and settle. That settle is precisely what reads as "alive". Every
"iOS-like" CSS easing floating around is an approximation that throws away the one
characteristic that matters.

**CSS `linear()` solves it.** It accepts an arbitrary sample list, so we solve the damped
harmonic oscillator and emit the real curve — overshoot included — while still running as a
normal CSS easing on the compositor. Confirmed supported here; all five parse and apply
with 43 stops intact.

| Preset | response | damping | overshoot | settles | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `.spring` | 0.55 | 0.825 | 1.0% | 758 ms | **Apple published** |
| `.interactiveSpring` | 0.15 | 0.86 | 0.5% | 210 ms | **Apple published** |
| `.smooth` | 0.40 | 1.00 | none | 588 ms | fitted to documented behaviour |
| `.snappy` | 0.35 | 0.85 | 0.6% | 488 ms | fitted |
| `.bouncy` | 0.45 | 0.65 | 6.8% | 742 ms | fitted |

Apple publishes the first two. For `.smooth` / `.snappy` / `.bouncy` it documents only the
*behaviour* — no overshoot / slight / clearly visible — so those three are fitted to
reproduce that and are labelled `fitted` in the output. Do not cite them as spec.

Verified emitted curves: `.spring` peaks at 1.0102, `.bouncy` at 1.0679, `.smooth` at
exactly 1.0000. Fallback to `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)` via `@supports not` for WebViews
without `linear()`.

| # | Behaviour | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3b.1 | Spring easings with real overshoot | **DONE** | `motion.css`, generated not eyeballed |
| 3b.2 | Scroll-linked header shadow | **DONE** | alpha 0 → 0.42 over 60px. One passive listener, one custom property, no layout. |
| 3b.3 | Reduce Motion | **DONE** | Treated as *none*, not *less* |
| 3b.4 | Rubber-band overscroll | **PARTIAL** | `overscroll-behavior` controls chaining but not the curve; a faithful rubber band needs JS. |

## 4. Platform reality

| Capability | Status | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `backdrop-filter` | supported | baseline works |
| **SVG filter as `backdrop-filter`** | **Chromium only** | The refraction only exists because Android WebView is Chromium. It would be impossible in Safari/Firefox. |
| `corner-shape: squircle` | supported (Chromium) | real superellipse corners |
| `mask-composite` | supported | the rim ring technique |
| `DeviceOrientationEvent` | varies | **In the app, don't use it** — the native bridge already streams gyro. |

⚠️ **minSdk 24.** Old WebViews will not have SVG-as-backdrop-filter. Feature-detect with
`CSS.supports('backdrop-filter','url(#x)')` and fall back to plain blur+saturate.

## 4b. ON-DEVICE RESULTS — Redmi Note 13 (2311DRK48I), measured 2026-07-28

Device: MediaTek `mt6897`, Android 16 / API 36, 1220×2712 @ 480dpi (dpr 3),
**WebView 150.0.7871.46**, Chrome 150. Driven over adb + Chrome DevTools Protocol.

### Feature support — everything needed is present
`backdrop-filter` ✓ · **SVG `url()` as backdrop-filter ✓** · `corner-shape: squircle` ✓ ·
`linear()` easing ✓ · `mask-composite` ✓ · `var()` inside `rgba()` ✓

All five generated spring easings parse on-device as real `linear()` with all 43 stops.

### 🚨 The headline: refraction is FREE on this device
| Filter on an animating panel | fps |
|---|---|
| none | 61 |
| `blur(2px) saturate(180%)` | 62 |
| `blur(24px) saturate(180%)` | 61 |
| **full Snell refraction** | **62** |

Display ceiling is 60Hz, so every mode is pinned at the ceiling — the refraction
filter is not the bottleneck at all.

| Simultaneous animating refracting surfaces | fps |
|---|---|
| 1 | 61 |
| 3 | 59 |
| 6 | 60 |
| 10 | **60** |

**Ten** at once still holds 60. This contradicts `CLAUDE.md` §7's "max 6 backdrop-filter
surfaces" on this chipset. Do NOT relax the rule blindly though — this was a static test
page. The app adds a live gameplay scene, continuous multi-touch, and the native engine
competing for the same GPU.

Gyro specular animation with 3 glass panels: **61 fps**. Free, as designed.

### The real cost is CPU map build, not GPU
| Surface | desktop | **device** | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| HUD 200×36 | 6.0 ms | **23.4 ms** | 3.9× |
| panel 280×170 | 13.8 ms | **35.2 ms** | 2.6× |
| sheet 380×520 | 32.8 ms | **54.2 ms** | 1.7× |
| full 406×864 | 27.8 ms | **69.9 ms** | 2.5× |

Even the smallest surface costs **more than one frame** to build. Build once at mount —
a single hitch — and never again. **`toDataURL` PNG encoding is 18–28 ms of that**, often
more than the physics itself. Worth testing `toBlob` + `createObjectURL` as a faster path.

### ⚠️ DeviceOrientation fires at only ~17 Hz
43 events in 2.5 s. Far below 60 fps, so the highlight would visibly step if driven from
the web API. **Use the native gyro bridge instead** — the app already streams it at a much
higher rate on a dedicated HandlerThread. This is now evidence, not a preference.

## 5. Cost, measured (desktop, warm, median of 5)

| Surface | Map build | vs 16.7 ms frame |
|---|---|---|
| HUD pill 200×36 | 6.0 ms | fits |
| panel 280×170 | 13.8 ms | fits, barely |
| sheet 380×520 | 32.8 ms | **blows the frame** |
| full 412×900 | 27.8 ms | **blows the frame** |

A mid-range phone will be several times slower. Rules that follow directly:

1. Build the map **once**, at mount. Never on resize, never per frame.
2. A glass surface **must not animate its size or shape** — that forces a rebuild.
   This is exactly what `CLAUDE.md` §7 already says; now it has a number behind it.
3. To animate the *strength* of refraction, animate the filter's `scale` attribute only.
4. **Never on a pad widget.** Unchanged and non-negotiable.

**Not yet measured: per-frame GPU compositing cost.** `requestAnimationFrame` is frozen in
the Browser pane (`document.hidden`), so FPS benchmarks time out rather than returning a
wrong number. This must be measured on the real phone.

## 6. Accessibility — hard limits

- **Specular amplitude ≤ 6 px.** Above ~10 px it stops reading as a highlight and becomes a
  moving object; it is also a vestibular trigger. The lab defaults to 6.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` → parallax **off entirely**, not merely reduced.
- `prefers-reduced-transparency` → solid surface, no backdrop-filter.
- 4.5:1 body-text contrast, hardest to hold on glass, which is exactly where glass puts you.
- Never signal state by colour alone.

## 8. Honest summary

With the split architecture, an Android WebView can reach **most** of the material: real
lensing, correct bezel geometry, motion-tracked highlights, gel press, size-driven
thickness. What stays out of reach is everything requiring **live framebuffer reads** —
true per-component light/dark adaptation and genuine colour spill.

The gap that remains is smaller than the gap between Apple's material and the
`feTurbulence` fake that most web implementations ship.
